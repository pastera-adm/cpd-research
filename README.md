## 📌 Overview

The **Curvature-Packing-Dynamics (CPD)** framework introduces a unified geometric language to describe how complex systems form, maintain, and transform internal structure. 

Across scientific disciplines, self-organizing patterns exhibit striking similarities—whether in chemical spirals, turbulent vortices, biological growth fronts, or strategic tension in games like chess. Traditional approaches rely on domain-specific mathematics (differential equations, payoff matrices, or reaction schemes). CPD uncovers the deeper, underlying geometry shared by all of them.

Rather than treating space as an empty stage or using abstract mathematical fictions, CPD defines three fundamental interacting fields that govern emergence across physical, informational, biological, and strategic environments.

## ⚙️ Core Theory: The Universal Triad ($C, P, Q$)

At the heart of the CPD framework is a minimal interaction cycle between three primitive fields:

1. **Curvature $C(x,t)$:** The local shape, potential imbalance, or directional incentive of a landscape. It dictates where a system *wants* to move, concentrate, or accumulate.
2. **Packing $P(x,t)$:** The local density, occupancy, crowding, or mechanical strain[cite: 1]. Regions of high $P$ act as local resistance, slowing or redirecting flow[cite: 1].
3. **Flux $Q(x,t)$:** The dynamical transport of material, energy, probability, or strategic pressure driven by $C$ and modulated/suppressed by $P$[cite: 1].
